WASH · COMMUNITY RESILIENCE Garissa County

Clean water, and the dignity it brings.

In one of Kenya’s driest counties, water decides everything — a mother’s safety, a child’s health, a community’s future. Fountain of Hope works for clean, safe water, and the resilience that comes with it.

Read the story All topics
Scroll

Clean water is the quiet foundation of almost everything else — a mother washing her hands beside a skilled birth attendant, a girl staying in school without a long walk to a distant borehole, a family surviving the drought cycles that punctuate Garissa’s calendar year. Water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) work is community resilience work.

The story

Water that works for everyone.

Access to clean water protects mothers during pregnancy and delivery, keeps children free of water-borne illness, and spares girls the daily burden that keeps them out of school.

— The story —

Why water is health.

Clean water is the quiet foundation of health: safer deliveries, healthier children, less illness — and dignity for the women and girls who carry daily water in communities across the county.

The discussion, unpacked

Water is resilience.

In a drought-prone county, a drop of clean water protects the health of everyone it reaches.

01 Water, mothers and newborns

A safe delivery needs clean water at every step — in the home, the health facility and the community. WASH reduces the infections that claim mothers and newborns, and makes every health service safer and more dignified.

02 Water, girls and school

The daily walk
Without nearby water, girls carry the burden of collection — a burden that competes with the school day.
Sanitation & dignity
Safe facilities and clean water make school a place where girls can manage hygiene and menstruation with dignity.
Resilience through drought
Sustainable water access protects households when the rains fail — and protects the gains of every other program.

03 Real change is built from within

Fountain of Hope works with communities, not for them — strengthening local management of water and natural resources so that the change outlasts any single project. That is resilience: a community that can meet its own next challenge.
